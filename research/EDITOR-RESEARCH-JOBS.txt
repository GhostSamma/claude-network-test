EDITOR RESEARCH — 10 JOBS + RULES
==================================

Written by Content for the AI video editor's taste/judgement layer.
Saved here so any session with YouTube access can run them as written.

STATUS (2026-10-03): these jobs require pulling YouTube transcripts with
timestamps. The cloud session cannot — YouTube bot-walls its datacenter IP
(LOGIN_REQUIRED). Run these from a machine with a residential connection.

======================================================================
RULES — APPLY TO EVERY JOB, NOT NEGOTIABLE
======================================================================

WHAT THIS IS FOR
I have an AI video editor that knows the mechanics of editing but has no taste
and no judgement. It cannot look at raw footage and say "this is a car edit, so
it needs this treatment." It does not know what the best work in a style looks
like. It does not know where a master would cut or why. You are going to go
find that out from the people who actually do it, in their own words, and write
it down.

THE EVIDENCE RULE — THIS IS THE WHOLE JOB
Every single claim you write must carry ONE of these:
  - a named creator + the video URL + a timestamp, or
  - a verbatim quote from a transcript + the video URL + a timestamp.
No exceptions. If you cannot source a claim, you write the words NOT FOUND and
move on.

Never write generic editing advice. "Use smooth transitions" and "match the
beat" are worthless to me — I already have that. I need "at 0:47 in [URL],
[creator] cuts on the wheel leaving frame, and at 2:13 of [tutorial URL] he
explains he does that because the eye is already travelling." If a section of
your output has no URL and no timestamp in it, delete the section.

DO NOT GUESS. DO NOT INFER. DO NOT FILL GAPS.
If the pros do not say it, it does not go in. An honest "NOT FOUND" is worth
more to me than a plausible sentence, because a plausible sentence gets taught
to my editor as fact and I will not be able to tell it apart from the real
findings later.

OUTPUT FORMAT — plain text only, no markdown tables, no fancy formatting
  - ONE file per agent. Name it with your job number, e.g.
    JOB-3-car-shot-breakdown.txt
  - Each section = ONE idea, under its own plain heading, 300 words maximum.
  - Tag every section with where it belongs, using exactly one of these:
        [FOUNDATION]            - a law that is true for every style of edit
        [CRAFT: transitions]    - or ramps / grade / effects / workflow
        [STYLE: car]            - or gaming
  - A section with no tag is unusable. Tag everything.

WHY THE FORMAT MATTERS: a human files these into the editor's memory
afterwards, one idea per note. A 900-word section covering four ideas has to be
taken apart by hand. Keep each one to a single idea and it files straight in.

YOUTUBE: you can pull video transcripts. Use that heavily — the pros explain
themselves in tutorials, streams and podcasts, and the transcript is where
their actual words are.

JOB 1 of 10 — Car edits: how a pro decides, in order
(Content's item A — Sean's number one ask)
======================================================================

Topic: the order a professional CAR-EDIT editor makes decisions in.

Find working car-edit editors explaining their own process - tutorials,
streams, podcasts, BTS videos, "how I made this" breakdowns. I want their
decision ORDER, in their words:
  footage is open in front of them -> how do they decide what TYPE of edit this
  is? -> how do they pick the style? -> when do they choose the song, before or
  after? -> what do they plan before touching a cut? -> what order do cuts,
  effects, grade, polish happen in?
For EACH step: the creator's name, the video URL, the timestamp, and a verbatim
quote of them saying it. If two pros contradict each other, write BOTH and say
they disagree - do not average them into one answer.
What I specifically need to know and cannot find out myself:
  - does the song come first or the footage? Do they all agree?
  - what do they look at in the raw footage in the first 60 seconds?
  - how do they decide a clip is unusable?
  - at what point is it too late to change the style?
Tag sections [FOUNDATION] if the step is true for any edit, [STYLE: car] if it
is car-specific, [CRAFT: workflow] for ordering.

JOB 2 of 10 — Car: shot-by-shot breakdown of benchmark PHONK MONTAGE edits
(Content's item B, first half)
======================================================================

Topic: pull apart 3 to 5 of the best car PHONK MONTAGE edits, cut by cut.

Pick edits that are genuinely benchmark - high views, widely imitated, named
creators. For each edit give me: creator, URL, view count. Then walk the edit
cut by cut, in plain text lines, one line per cut:
  timestamp | what is on screen | what the camera is doing | the transition
  INTO this shot and how it is built | any effect and HOW LONG IT HOLDS | why
  this shot follows the last one
Go for at least the first 30 seconds of each edit, cut by cut. More if you can.
The three things my editor gets wrong, so dig hardest here:
  1. WHERE to cut. It cuts on the beat and nothing else. What else are these
     pros cutting on - a movement finishing, a light passing, the car leaving
     frame?
  2. Effects that VANISH. My editor adds an effect and it is gone in 2 frames.
     How long do the pros actually hold one?
  3. Shots that do not CONNECT. What makes shot 7 feel like it belongs after
     shot 6?
Name the creator and URL and timestamp on every single line. If you cannot see
what the camera is doing, write UNCLEAR rather than guessing.
Tag [STYLE: car] and [CRAFT: transitions] / [CRAFT: effects] as appropriate.

JOB 3 of 10 — Car: shot-by-shot breakdown of benchmark CINEMATIC SHOWCASE edits
(Content's item B, second half)
======================================================================

Same method as the phonk job, different style.

Pull apart 3 to 5 benchmark CAR CINEMATIC / SHOWCASE edits - the slower,
moodier, commercial-looking kind, not the fast phonk montages. Named creators,
URLs, view counts. Same cut-by-cut lines: timestamp | on screen | camera move |
transition in and how built | effect and how long it holds | why it follows the
previous shot. At least the first 30 seconds of each.
What I need from this one specifically: these edits hold shots far longer than
a montage does. How long? What is happening inside a long shot to keep it alive
- a slow push, a light moving, a reflection travelling? My editor cannot hold a
shot without it feeling dead.
And: how do they get away with so few cuts? What carries the edit when cutting
is not?
Name creator, URL, timestamp on every line. UNCLEAR where you cannot tell.
Tag [STYLE: car] and [CRAFT: transitions] / [CRAFT: ramps] as appropriate.

JOB 4 of 10 — Car: how pros make effects feel INTEGRATED, not pasted on
(Content's item C)
======================================================================

Topic: how professional car editors make an effect look like it belongs in the
shot rather than stuck on top of it.

This is my editor's worst habit - every effect looks pasted on. Find tutorials
where pros build these, and write down the actual STEPS with timestamps:
  - rim lights and glows that RIDE the wheel or the body line as the car moves
    (tracked, not a static overlay)
  - speed ramps timed to a REAL camera move, not applied at random
  - the car masked off the background so it pops, with the background graded
    separately
  - transitions that go in AND out seamlessly, where you cannot see the join
  - light leaks, reflections, flares that move with the shot
For each: creator, tutorial URL, timestamp, and the steps in order. If a pro
states a VALUE - a frame count, a blur amount, a scale percentage, a duration -
write the exact number down and where they said it. Those numbers are the most
useful thing you can bring me and they are almost never in writing.
Also: anything a pro explicitly says NOT to do. Those are gold.
Tag [CRAFT: effects] mostly, [STYLE: car] where it only applies to cars.

JOB 5 of 10 — Car: the competition
(Content's item F)
======================================================================

Topic: who are the best car-edit creators working right now, and what do they
each do that is recognisably theirs?

I need to know who my editor is up against. For each creator - aim for 8 to 12
- give me:
  - name and channel URL
  - roughly how big they are
  - their SIGNATURE: the thing you could identify their work by without being
    told
  - 2 or 3 of their best edits, by URL, with what makes each one good
  - specifically: what do they do that an average car editor does NOT do?
The last one is the point of this job. I want the gap between good and average
named concretely, with examples, not described in adjectives.
Also tell me which of them explain their own methods publicly, with links -
those channels are where the next round of research goes.
Every claim gets a URL. Where you cannot verify a follower count or a view
count, say UNVERIFIED rather than estimating.
Tag [STYLE: car].

JOB 6 of 10 — Gaming: decision order + shot-by-shot breakdowns
(Content's A+B for gaming)
======================================================================

Topic: gaming montage editing - how the pros decide, and how the best ones are
actually cut.

Two halves.
HALF ONE: the decision order, from gaming editors' own words. Footage open ->
how do they decide what kind of montage this is -> song choice, before or after
the clips -> what gets planned -> what order do cuts, effects, grade happen.
Creator, URL, timestamp, verbatim quote for each step. Note any disagreement
between pros rather than resolving it.
HALF TWO: pull apart 2 to 3 benchmark gaming montages cut by cut, same line
format as the car jobs: timestamp | on screen | camera or player movement |
transition in and how built | effect and how long it holds | why it follows the
last shot. First 30 seconds minimum.
Gaming-specific things I need: how do they handle the kill moment - cut on it,
before it, after it? What do they do with the boring footage between kills? How
do they use the game's own camera movement as part of the edit?
Everything sourced. NOT FOUND where it is not.
Tag [STYLE: gaming], plus [CRAFT: transitions] / [CRAFT: workflow].

JOB 7 of 10 — Gaming: integrated effects + the competition
(Content's C+F for gaming)
======================================================================

Topic: gaming montage effects that look built-in, and who the best creators
are.

Two halves.
HALF ONE: how pro gaming editors make effects feel integrated rather than
pasted. Tracked elements that follow the player or the crosshair, shake and
zoom tied to real impacts, transitions hidden inside muzzle flashes or
explosions, colour grading that sells the mood. Tutorial URLs with timestamps
and the actual steps. Exact numbers wherever a pro states one - frame counts,
shake amounts, durations. Also anything they say explicitly not to do.
HALF TWO: the top gaming montage editors right now. 8 to 12 of them: name,
channel URL, rough size, their signature look, 2 to 3 best edits by URL, and
the concrete thing they do that an average gaming editor does not. Name which
ones teach their methods publicly.
Same evidence rule throughout. UNVERIFIED for numbers you cannot confirm.
Tag [STYLE: gaming] and [CRAFT: effects].

JOB 8 of 10 — How a pro recognises a style from the footage alone
(Content's cross-style job — the one Sean described best)
======================================================================

Topic: how an experienced editor looks at raw footage and knows what kind of
edit it wants.

This is the single thing my editor cannot do, so this job matters most. Sean
described it like this: a master sits down, looks at the footage, and says "ok
this is this type of video so it needs this type of edit style."
Find editors - any discipline, not just car and gaming - explaining how they
make that read. What are they looking at? Shot length? How much camera movement
there is? Light? Subject? How much coverage they have? Whether the footage is
handheld or gimbal or static?
I want the CUES, named, with the creator who named them, the URL and the
timestamp. A cue is something you can point at in the footage: "if the clips
are all under two seconds and handheld, it wants a fast cut." Not "you develop
an instinct for it" - that is useless to me.
Also: what do pros say about choosing the WRONG style, and how do they realise
it has happened? Recovery stories are useful.
This one is likely to be thin and heavily quoted rather than comprehensive.
That is fine. Depth over breadth, and NOT FOUND where it is not there. Do not
pad this job.
Tag [FOUNDATION] for anything true across styles.

JOB 9 of 10 — Overdone vs fresh, and how pros build a signature
(Content's D+E)
======================================================================

Topic: what is played out in car and gaming edits right now, what still lands,
and how named pros twist shared effects into something of their own.

HALF ONE - overdone vs fresh, for 2025 and 2026. I need EVIDENCE, not opinion:
  - creators saying outright that an effect is overused (quote, URL, timestamp)
  - comment sections where viewers call something tired - describe the pattern
    and link it
  - effects that keep showing up in the newest highly-viewed work
Say clearly which of these is a creator's stated view and which is your read of
a comment pattern. Do not blend them.
HALF TWO - creativity. Take 4 or 5 named pros who use the SAME common effect - a
speed ramp, a whip transition, a glow - and show how each one does it
differently enough to be recognisably theirs. URLs and timestamps side by side
so the difference is visible.
That second half is the real goal: my editor can copy an effect but cannot vary
it into a voice. I want proof that the same effect has many correct versions,
with examples.
Tag [FOUNDATION] for the signature material, [STYLE: car] / [STYLE: gaming]
for the overdone lists.

This job is mechanical and it is valuable. Pull full transcripts from these

  - Camilo Castañeda  (@camicees)
  - Jordan Orme

Then find and pull transcripts from any other top car-edit or gaming-edit

Aim for breadth - as many teaching videos as you can get through. This is the
raw material the other nine jobs are reading from, and anything you pull here
is reusable later.
No tags needed on this one - it is source material, not findings.


======================================================================
NOTES FOR SEAN (from Content)
======================================================================

Why it is shaped this way. Every brief is self-contained — the agent never
needs to ask us anything.

The one rule doing the heavy lifting is named creator + URL + timestamp, or
NOT FOUND. Without it, research like this comes back as blog advice that reads
fine and teaches the editor nothing. With it, a claim that is not real cannot
survive to the page.

Job 10 is the sleeper. Raw transcripts of the pros teaching, in their own
words, are reusable for months and cost us nothing to keep.

Job 8 is the one Sean cares most about and the one most likely to come back
thin, because pros rarely articulate the read. Thin-but-sourced is the right
outcome there; the brief tells it not to pad.

What was not done: no insect, food, anime or cinematic-AI styles. Content's
call — those seats are not live.

Returns come back as plain text files. Content has the editor file them into
its levels.
