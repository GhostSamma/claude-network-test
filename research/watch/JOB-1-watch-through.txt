FINDING 1. The measured shape of a short-video retention curve (Kuaishou, 4.7M views)
KuaiRec small matrix: 1,411 users each shown all 3,327 videos, 4,676,570 views, Kuaishou, July-September 2020. Median video length 9.6 s (5th to 95th percentile 5.9 to 45.1 s). The dataset gives play_duration and video_duration per view. Computed by me, share of views whose total play time reached t seconds: 1 s 98.1%, 2 s 95.0%, 3 s 92.2%, 5 s 81.5%, 8 s 43.1%, 10 s 23.4%, 15 s 5.7%, 30 s 1.1%. For videos 30 s or longer, the share of remaining viewers who stop in each second is 1.9% (second 1), 3.7%, 3.1%, 4.5%, 7.8%, 11.3%, 19.5%, then 26% to 30% per second through second 12. So the curve is not a steep early drop then a long tail. It is a shoulder, then a fall between seconds 5 and 10. Median watch ratio 0.77; 32.4% of views reach 1.0 times the video length (loops count); 4.7% reach 2.0 times.
Caveats: play_duration is total time on the video, not position, so a rewind or loop adds time. This is 2020 Kuaishou with roughly 10 s clips, not 30 to 60 s Shorts. The paper does not explain the 8 to 12 s hazard peak; do not treat it as a law.
Dataset page field text: "watch_ratio The video watching ratio (=play_duration/video_duration)."
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRec v2 (Zenodo 18164998, files at https://zenodo.org/records/18164998; data card https://kuairec.com), paper Gao et al., KuaiRec: A Fully-observed Dataset and Insights for Evaluating Recommender Systems, CIKM 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10842
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. By design every one of the 1,411 users watched every video, so viewer mix is identical across videos. Topic is not controlled here (see finding 4 for the within-category check). Editing is not measured at all; the file has no video content.

FINDING 2. Is there a cliff, and at what second? It depends on who is shown the video, not on a fixed second
Computed by me. Kuaishou, three exposure conditions, share of views that ended before:
Fully observed matrix (KuaiRec small, 4,676,570 views): 1 s 1.9%, 2 s 5.0%, 3 s 7.8%, 5 s 18.5%.
Natural recommended feed (KuaiRec big matrix, 7,176 users, 12,530,806 views): 1 s 5.3%, 2 s 12.5%, 3 s 18.4%, 5 s 30.0%.
Randomly inserted videos (KuaiRand-Pure random log, 27,285 users, 1,149,139 views with positive duration, April-May 2022): 1 s 9.2%, 2 s 47.5%, 3 s 63.0%, 5 s 76.5%; median 2.1 s. For videos 30 s or longer the single most common stop time is 1.0 to 1.5 s (23% of views of such videos). In the recommender-selected rows of the same file the median is 4.8 s and 31.8% end before 2 s.
So a sharp early cliff appears when viewers are shown something chosen at random, and is mild when the feed or the researchers matched the video to the viewer. I found no study that locates one universal cliff second.
Warning: KuaiRand mixes two app interfaces (two-column and single-column) and the file does not label which row is which (the tab column is only described as "indicating the scenario of this interaction"), so its seconds are not clean swipe-feed numbers.
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRec https://zenodo.org/records/18164998 ; KuaiRand https://zenodo.org/records/10439422 and https://kuairand.com ; paper Gao et al., KuaiRand, CIKM 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08696
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. The random-insertion design in KuaiRand removes the recommender's choice of video for viewer, which isolates match from everything else; it does not separate topic from edit.

FINDING 3. What real TikTok users do: two donation studies disagree by a wide margin
Zannettou et al. (CHI 2024): 347 recruited users, 9.2M TikTok views (July 2020 to February 2022). Watch time was inferred from gaps between timestamps in donated browsing history. Result: "for 45% of the video views, the participants watched the videos until the end, while for the rest 55% of the video views, the participants proceeded to the next video before its completion." Also "24% of the video views are skipped quickly (i.e., before watching 20% of the entire video duration), and 40% of the video views are skipped before watching 50% of the video." Median attention 82%. No participant watched more than 65% of their videos to the end; 70% of participants finished 30% to 50% of their videos.
Masood et al. (CHI 2026): data donation, 3,870,540 view records used for a model. They set the threshold at 10% watched because it "yields a balanced classification problem, i.e., 50% of videos in our dataset were watched over 10%." That implies a median watch fraction near 10%, versus 82% in the first study. Both infer watch time from browsing-history timestamps; the gap is unresolved in the papers. Treat any single TikTok average watch-through figure as method-dependent.
Neither study reports watch-through by video length or by edit features.
SOURCE: [PEER-REVIEWED] Zannettou et al., Analyzing User Engagement with TikTok's Short Format Video Recommendations using Data Donations, CHI 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04945 ; [PEER-REVIEWED] Masood et al., Counting How the Seconds Count, CHI 2026 (per arXiv listing), https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20030
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. Observational viewing histories; no control for topic, creator or edit.

FINDING 4. Video length versus watch-through, Kuaishou: the percentage falls with length, the seconds do not
Computed by me, KuaiRec small matrix (viewer set identical across rows). By video length: mean watch ratio, share of views reaching full length, mean seconds actually watched.
5 to 7 s: 1.37, 69%, 8.5 s. 9 to 11 s: 0.87, 25%, 8.7 s. 13 to 15 s: 0.63, 8%, 8.8 s. 20 to 30 s: 0.36, 2.1%, 8.7 s. 30 to 60 s: 0.20, 0.7%, 8.7 s. Over 60 s: 0.08, 0.3%, 8.8 s.
Across the 3,327 videos, rank correlation of length with mean watch ratio is -0.987. Twenty length bins alone explain 94% of the variance in per-video mean watch ratio; the 30 first-listed category tags explain 14%. Within each of the 17 tag groups with at least 50 videos, the rank correlation of length with mean watch ratio is between -0.97 and -1.00, so the result holds inside topic. Per-video mean seconds watched sit between 8.0 and 9.4 s for 90% of videos.
In the natural-feed matrix seconds rise only modestly with length (7.9 s for clips under 5 s to 10.5 s over 60 s) and the correlation of length with mean watch ratio is -0.82.
Meaning: a 7 s clip and a 45 s clip are not comparable on watch-through; the longer one cannot score high unless people stay several times longer than they do on short ones.
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRec https://zenodo.org/records/18164998 (files small_matrix.csv, big_matrix.csv, item_categories.csv); paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10842
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Viewers held fixed by design; topic partly controlled through coarse category tags inside the analysis. Fine topic and edit style are not controlled.

FINDING 5. Length versus watch-through, YouTube, 5.3 million videos (long form, not Shorts)
Wu, Rizoiu and Xie (ICWSM 2018): 5,331,204 YouTube videos published July to August 2016 from 1,257,412 channels; median length 5 minutes. "In the Tweeted videos dataset, duration alone explains more than 58% of the variance of watch percentage." They show the relation is non-linear and publish an "engagement map" of length versus watch percentage, and define relative engagement as the rank of a video's watch percentage inside its length bin, so length is removed. One channel's short videos averaged 0.82 watched and its long videos 0.21, but relative engagement was 0.92 and 0.78. Predicting watch percentage before upload from length, channel and topic metadata reaches R-squared 0.77 (0.45 for relative engagement); these predictors include no editing features.
Park, Naaman and Berger (ICWSM 2016), 1,125 random YouTube videos plus 1,814 videos in viewer logs: "shorter videos were watched much longer in terms of proportion of the video viewed."
Limits: long-form, 2016, aggregate per video not per view. This is the one large study that explicitly strips length out; its quantity is relative engagement, which no platform reports to creators.
SOURCE: [PEER-REVIEWED] Wu et al., Beyond Views: Measuring and Predicting Engagement in Online Videos, ICWSM 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.02541 ; [PEER-REVIEWED] Park, Naaman, Berger, A Data-Driven Study of View Duration on YouTube, ICWSM 2016, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14781
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Length is separated by construction (binning); topic and channel are only controlled as regression features in the prediction task, and edit is not measured.

FINDING 6. The benchmark numbers that circulate are vendor claims with no published method, and the testable one fails
All [VENDOR], unpublished. Humble and Brag blog: "A healthy average percentage viewed for Shorts in 2026 is 70 per cent or above" and "hold above 80 per cent in the first three seconds". Prepublish blog: "over 70% of the viewers who leave do it within the first 2 seconds". Opus Pro blog: "When I analyzed 500 videos across different niches... videos with 70% average watch time received 4.3 times more impressions than those with 40% retention" (no data, no method, no control for topic or channel size). Wistia (13 million business videos, hosted on its own player, not social feeds): "Videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate."
Test against measured data (computed by me): of Kuaishou views that did not reach full length, only 7.4% (fully observed matrix) and 18.9% (natural feed) ended before 2 seconds, not 70%. Platform differs (Kuaishou 2020, 10 s clips), so this is a contradiction of the claim as a general law, not a measurement of Shorts.
No YouTube help page I fetched gives a 70% threshold (see finding 9).
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks ; https://prepublish.ai/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts ; https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data ; https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length ; [DATASET] KuaiRec https://zenodo.org/records/18164998 (counter-test)
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. None of the vendor figures controls topic, channel size, length or audience; the Opus comparison is explicitly uncontrolled.

FINDING 7. Viewer versus video: how much of watch time the video itself explains
Computed by me, KuaiRand-Pure random log (videos inserted at random, 27,285 users, 7,344 videos, 1,149,139 views, median 162 views per video). Share of view-level variance explained (adjusted R-squared):
log seconds watched: viewer ID alone 20.2%, video ID alone 5.5%, both 25.7%, video length alone 0.1%.
completion fraction (play over length, capped at 1): viewer 8.5%, video 22.0%, both 30.5%, length alone 18.2%.
So about 83% of what the video explains in completion is just its length; the video-specific remainder (topic, edit, creator, thumbnail all bundled) is about 4 points of per-view variance. KuaiRec small matrix agrees: watch ratio is 1.8% viewer, 7.3% video; seconds watched is 2.1% viewer, 0.2% video.
Do not read this as meaning editing does not matter: per-view variance is mostly noise. Averaged over 50 or more views, per-video mean seconds watched in KuaiRand ranged from 3.1 s (5th percentile) to 14.7 s (95th), mean completion from 1.7% to 36%. Videos do differ; the study cannot say why.
Also Masood et al.: predicting whether a viewer watches more than 10% of a video reached 69.61% accuracy; removing the video-content embedding cost only 3.13 points.
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRand https://zenodo.org/records/10439422 ; KuaiRec https://zenodo.org/records/18164998 ; [PEER-REVIEWED] Masood et al., CHI 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20030
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Random exposure separates viewer from video; "video" still bundles length, topic, edit and creator. No edit feature was extracted.

FINDING 8. How Kuaishou labels a good view: absolute seconds, capped, not a percentage
KuaiRand data card: valid_play "equals 1 when: play_time_ms >= duration_ms if duration_ms <= 7,000 ms , or play_time_ms > 7,000 ms if duration_ms > 7,000 ms." long_view "equals 1 when: play_time_ms >= duration_ms if duration_ms <= 18,000 ms , or play_time_ms >=18,000 ms if duration_ms > 18,000 ms." So on this platform a view of a long video counts as a success after 7 s (or 18 s) regardless of length, and short clips must be finished. My reading, not the authors': for clips longer than 7 s or 18 s the label no longer depends on percentage watched. The KuaiRec authors label a strong-preference view as one where "the user's cumulative watching time of this video is greater than twice the duration of this video", a rewatch-based label asserted by Kuaishou's analysts, not tested.
These are labels in a research release, not a statement of how ranking weights them.
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRand data card https://kuairand.com ; KuaiRec paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10842
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. These are definitions, not an effect estimate.

FINDING 10. Does watch-through drive reach? What platforms say (assertions, not tests)
TikTok newsroom: "A strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator, such as whether the video's viewer and creator are both in the same country." No weights or numbers are given, and the example is about the viewer's interest, not the creator's reach.
Instagram ranking explainer, Reels: "The most important predictions we make are how likely you are to reshare a reel, watch a reel all the way through, like it, and go to the audio page." Same page, editor-relevant and documented: "We aim to make certain reels less visible for other reasons, such as low-resolution or watermarked reels, reels that are muted or contain borders, reels that are majority text, or reels that have already been posted on Instagram."
The YouTube analytics pages I fetched contain no ranking statement.
These are stated objectives per viewer. None says that a higher completion rate raises a creator's number of impressions, and none says how it trades off against length.
NOT FOUND: any platform document stating how watch-through changes a video's distribution to new audiences.

FINDING 11. Does watch-through drive reach? The independent tests found, all viewer-side or observational
Boeker and Urman (WWW 2022): sock-puppet audit of TikTok, 39 successfully completed runs, each with an active bot and an identical control bot, varying only the share of video watched (25% to 400%). Result: watch behavior changed that viewer's own feed, though the authors say its influence "is only marginally higher than the one of the like-feature", and "Contrary to our assumptions" watching 25% moved the feed more than watching 75% (0.85% versus 0.56% averaged difference). Pooled slope of feed difference: 0.42% to 0.59% for controls, 0.75% to 0.98% for watch scenarios. Viewer's feed only, not creator reach.
Zhan et al. (KDD 2022, Kuaishou, 1.35 billion samples): the platform's watch-time goal pushed impressions toward long videos: "due to the platform's goal of maximizing user watch time". Live A/B, 5% of users per arm: their duration-adjusted model (Res-D2Q) versus the plain watch-time regression baseline gave +0.746% watch time, interval plus or minus 0.15%.
Wu et al.: engagement and popularity separate: top-n most viewed versus most watched videos correlate 0.48 at n=50 and 0.08 at n=500. Zannettou et al. found 55% of recommended views are not finished and read that as TikTok not maximizing completion (their inference).
NOT FOUND: a creator-side study, randomized or observational with controls, showing watch-through causing more reach.
SOURCE: [PEER-REVIEWED] Boeker and Urman, An Empirical Investigation of Personalization Factors on TikTok, WWW 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12271 ; [PEER-REVIEWED] Zhan et al., Deconfounding Duration Bias in Watch-time Prediction for Video Recommendation, KDD 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06003
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Boeker and Urman use matched control bots, so topic is held constant; the others are observational.

FINDING 12. What watch-through is a proxy for: positive actions rise with it under random exposure
Computed by me, KuaiRand-Pure random log (1,149,139 views). Rate of actions by watch ratio band (band size in views): under 0.25 (996,941): like 0.27%, follow 0.014%, comment 0.008%, forward 0.020%. 0.25 to 0.5 (65,179): like 1.12%. 0.5 to 1 (48,883): like 1.95%. 1 to 2 (34,137): like 2.65%, follow 0.21%, comment 0.36%, forward 0.16%. Over 2 (3,999): like 3.08%, follow 0.30%, comment 1.53%, forward 0.48%. Dislike ("hate") does not rise with watch ratio.
This is the likely reason platforms use watch-through: it co-moves with likes, follows, comments and shares. It is correlation within a view; a like or comment needs time on the video, so the direction is partly reverse. There is no reach outcome in the file because exposure was random by design.
The same pattern holds in the recommender-selected rows (like 0.71% under 0.25 versus 4.30% at 1 to 2).
SOURCE: [DATASET] KuaiRand-Pure https://zenodo.org/records/10439422 ; paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08696
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Random exposure removes the recommender's selection; viewers still choose how long to watch.

FINDING 13. Loops and rewatches: how common, what platforms say, and no measured effect on distribution
Computed by me, KuaiRec: views at or above 1.0 times video length are 32.4% (fully observed) and 33.8% (natural feed); at or above 2.0 times, 4.7% and 7.5%. Looping depends on length: 69% of views of 5 to 7 s clips reach full length versus 0.7% of 30 to 60 s videos. Zhao et al. (KDD 2024): "both the proportion of users' repeat playing and the degree of repeat playing are higher for shorter videos and decrease as video duration increases" (KuaiRand and WeChat Channels).
YouTube: engaged views "not including any loops"; the retention page says segment views can exceed overall views because "within a single view the same viewer may watch portions of your content multiple times."
Boeker and Urman included 200% and 400% watch scenarios on TikTok, but report them pooled with other watch scenarios.
NOT FOUND: any study measuring whether loops or rewatches raise a video's distribution. A vendor post (Humble and Brag) calls replays "one of the strongest positive signals available"; it gave no data.

SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. Frequencies and platform definitions; no causal estimate.

FINDING 14. What data a single machine can use, and what is missing
KuaiRec (watch ratio, fully observed, 432 MB zip) and KuaiRand-Pure (47 MB, random exposure, 12 feedback fields) have per-view watch time but no video files. MicroLens (arXiv 2309.15379) has 1 billion interactions with raw video, audio and covers, but its interactions come "primarily through the collection of comment data", so it has no watch time and cannot be used for retention. Public performance sets I inspected carry views, not retention: Shofo TikTok General Small (50,000 videos with files, transcripts, "sticker_text", duration, play_count and like counts), TikTok-10M (10,000,000 posts, play_count, duration), YouTube Shorts dataset (1,077 clips, view_count, like_count). A Kaggle Shorts set has 300 videos with views, likes, comments, shares and duration.
NOT FOUND: any public per-video Shorts, Reels or TikTok dataset with measured watch time or retention. One Kaggle set (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/positivealexey/youtube-channel-performance-analytics) lists "Average View Percentage (%)"; its card does not say how many videos, which channels, or whether any are Shorts, so it is unverified.
NOT FOUND: any dataset or study in this job that links measured retention to measured editing features (cut rate, text overlays, sound) while controlling topic.

FINDING 15. Verdict: what this job found that the usual advice gets wrong or never tests
Verdict. Three things this job found that the usual advice gets wrong or never tests.
(a) Percent watched is mostly arithmetic on video length. In 4.7 million fully observed Kuaishou views the average viewer watched about 8.5 seconds whether the clip was 6 seconds or 60 seconds long, so the percentage collapses as clips get longer (sections 1, 4, 7).
(b) Viewer identity explains several times more of the seconds watched than the video does (completion percentage is the exception, and that is length), and what is left for the video (topic, edit, creator together) is small per view (section 7).
(c) No study found tests whether better watch-through gets a creator more reach. Platforms assert it. The independent tests are viewer-side (what a viewer's own feed becomes), not creator-side (section 10, 11).
Lead correction: arXiv 2202.10128 is NOT KuaiRec (it is a physics paper on cuprates). The real KuaiRec paper is arXiv 2202.10842.
