JOB 8 - FOLKLORE GRAVEYARD
TOPIC: Widely repeated short-video advice: where each belief came from, whether anything measured it, whether it was tested and failed
DATE: 2026-10-03 (every source below was fetched in this session; failed fetches are named)
BELIEFS EXAMINED: 10
NO MEASUREMENT BEHIND THE ADVISED NUMBER: 4 of 10 (cuts per N seconds, 8-second attention span, "use 3-5 hashtags", face in thumbnail). The other 6 rest on vendor or platform self-reports: mostly ad data or descriptive data, with no method published. None of the 10 has an independent controlled test on organic short video.


BELIEF 1: "The optimal video length is N seconds / keep it under 2 minutes"
Origin: Wistia [VENDOR], which sells video hosting and marketing software. Wistia 2012 (Ben Ruedlinger): "shorter videos are better for getting people to watch the whole thing", based on "average % viewed" of Wistia-hosted videos. No sample size is given. Wistia 2016 (Ezra Fishman, July 5, 2016; read via a PDF snapshot taken 1/17/2019 and hosted by TRU, because web.archive.org is not reachable from here): "we looked at 564,710 videos, and more than 1.3 billion plays ... we dove into our customer data". It concludes "2 minutes is the sweet spot". The chart's x-axis runs from 1 to 20 minutes, so it says nothing about 15 s versus 30 s versus 60 s. Wistia 2025 (same URL, now rewritten): "we analyzed over 13 million videos uploaded to Wistia". It finds "Videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate", which is lower than the roughly 70% the 2016 chart shows for 1-2 minutes. The vendor's own numbers move between editions.
Problems: "engagement" here means percent of length watched. That metric favours short videos by construction, and the data is marketing video embedded on business websites, not feed video. maestrolearning.com copies the 564,710 figure with no caveats.
Platform doc: YouTube Help: "There's no universal 'ideal' length for YouTube videos." Peer-reviewed work from inside a platform treats length as a confounder rather than a lever. Zhan et al. 2022 (Kuaishou, arXiv 2206.06003): "duration is a confounding factor that concurrently affects video exposure and watch-time prediction". Zheng et al. MM'22 (arXiv 2208.05190): "videos with longer duration tend to have longer watch time".
Verdict: measured by a vendor, on another medium, with a metric that favours short videos. No seconds-scale measurement for short video was found.
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://wistia.com/blog/does-length-matter-it-does-for-video-2k12-edition -> https://eddl.tru.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/EDDL5101_W5_Fisherman_2016.pdf (snapshot of https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length, 2016) -> https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length (2025) -> https://maestrolearning.com/blogs/how-long-should-your-video-be/ ; contra: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06003 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05190
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. Wistia compares videos of different lengths, which are also different content types (its own 2016 text: "Videos beyond 12 minutes are likely either narrative-driven stories or intense tutorials"). Nothing holds the content fixed.


BELIEF 2: "Post at the best time / day (e.g. Tue-Thu 2-6 pm)"
Sprout Social [VENDOR, sells social scheduling software]: "Tuesdays through Thursdays between 2 p.m. and 6 p.m." and "Worst day to post on TikTok: Sundays". Method: "2 billion social media engagements between November 27, 2025 and February 27, 2026 ... 307,000 global social media profiles across 30,000 Sprout Social customers."
Buffer [VENDOR, scheduler]: "The single best time to post is Sunday at 9 a.m." It says: "we looked at the median engagement rate across 7.1 million posts published through Buffer". It concedes: "Other studies have found stronger engagement on different days, which highlights how much results can vary by audience, dataset, and methodology."
Hootsuite [VENDOR, scheduler]: "Thursday morning (6 - 9 am) and Saturday midday", "Based on our experiments and an analysis of 1 million social posts". The "experiments" link goes to a 2021 test of Reels against TikTok (five videos per platform) that has nothing to do with posting time.
Later [VENDOR, scheduler]: "Monday to Friday at 10am PST", from "Later's social team" posting on its own account.
So four vendors give four incompatible answers, and Sunday is both the worst day (Sprout) and the best (Buffer). Every dataset is the vendor's own customers' posts. None controls for which accounts choose to post when. Each page ends by selling a "best time" feature.
Platform doc: YouTube describes time of day as a viewer signal, not an upload lever: "It learns from signals like time of day and device type to understand diverse viewing preferences."
Peer-reviewed test on short video: NOT FOUND (an arXiv search for "posting time" plus engagement returned nothing relevant; the Semantic Scholar API returned 429).
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-tiktok/ ; https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/ ; https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/ -> https://blog.hootsuite.com/experiment-reels-vs-tiktok/ ; https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/ ; platform: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. These are observational aggregates of customer posts. Account, content and audience vary along with posting hour, and no vendor states a control.


BELIEF 3: "Cut every 1-3 seconds / pattern interrupt every 3-5 seconds"
The chains:
edicionvideopro.com [VENDOR, sells editing services]: "Videos with pattern interrupts every 3-5 seconds show 40-60% higher average retention ... (EdicionVideoPro internal data, 200+ TikToks analyzed)". No method, no link, no data.
vidpros.com [VENDOR, video editing agency]: Reels "achieve 3.3 times higher completion rates when using rapid cuts every 0.5-1 second". It links to Social Media Examiner (Jan 2, 2025, Mari Smith and Michael Stelzner). That page was fetched and contains neither "3.3 times", "rapid cuts" nor "1.7 seconds". The cited source does not hold the claim.
influencers-time.com [content site]: "Data released in early 2025 by Influencer Marketing Hub shows that videos with an average shot change every 1-2 seconds experience retention rates up to 38% higher". No link. A web search finds this claim only on influencers-time itself. Origin NOT FOUND. The same page cites a "2025 TikTok Creative Center report" (24%) and "Social Video Insider" (29%), both unlinked and not found.
shortzly.com [VENDOR, clipping tool] says the opposite cadence: "Plan one deliberate interrupt every 10-15 seconds".
Academic test: NOT FOUND. arXiv searches for "cuts per second", "editing pace" and "shot length" plus engagement returned nothing relevant. BBC (2017) quotes the researcher behind film shot-length trends: shorter shots show only "that film-makers have got better at trying to grab our attention", not a measured effect on viewing.
Verdict: no measurement exists. The numbers disagree (0.5 s, 1-2 s, 3-5 s, 10-15 s) and none traces to data.
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://edicionvideopro.com/en/editing-for-platforms-video-marketing/pattern-interrupts-tiktok-retention-guide/ ; https://vidpros.com/video-clip-length/ -> https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-content-strategy-2025-whats-actually-working-right-now/ (claim absent) ; https://www.influencers-time.com/tiktok-editing-secrets-to-boost-retention-and-engagement/ (dead end) ; https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies ; https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? n/a - no measurement exists


BELIEF 4: "You have 3 seconds to hook them"
Origin 1: Facebook for Business, "Capture Attention with Updated Features for Video Ads" [PLATFORM DOC]. The page shows no date; AdExchanger and Social Media Today date it Feb 10-13, 2016. It says: "65% of people who watch the first three seconds of a video will watch for at least ten seconds and 45% continue watching for thirty seconds." Footnote 5 is attached, but the footnote text is not on the page. It is a conditional, descriptive figure about ads, with no comparison of strong and weak openings.
Origin 2: the Nielsen line links to "The Value of Video for Brands" [PLATFORM DOC, commissioned]: "Nielsen analyzed data from 173 of their BrandEffect studies ... people who watched under three seconds of the video ad created up to 47 percent of the total campaign value." That is ad brand lift from viewers who left early. It says nothing about retention, and if anything argues that three seconds is enough for an ad.
Origin 3: TikTok "9 Creative Tips" PDF for auction ads [PLATFORM DOC] (created 2020-11-12): "over 63% of all videos with the highest click-through rate (CTR) highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds." No base rate is given (what share of all ads do this?) and no method.
Mutation: houseofmarketers.com [VENDOR, agency] rewrites the Facebook figure as "45% ... will continue watching it for at least another 30 seconds". hansencommerce.com [VENDOR] turns TikTok's line into "hook viewers within the first three seconds".
Academic: Zhang et al. 2026 (arXiv 2602.22299) [PREPRINT] reports "correlations between hooking period features and key performance metrics like conversion per investment", again on ads.
SOURCE: [PLATFORM DOC] https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads -> https://www.facebook.com/business/news/value-of-video ; https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf ; repeaters https://www.socialmediatoday.com/social-business/facebook-adds-automated-captions-video-ads-offers-tips-improve-video-performance ; https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/ ; https://hansencommerce.com/insights-tiktok-hook-3-seconds ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22299
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. Every figure is descriptive or correlational on ads. Nobody varied the opening while holding the rest of the video fixed.


BELIEF 5: "9:16 vertical outperforms horizontal (9x / 90% higher completion)"
Origin 1: Snapchat 2015 [VENDOR, sells vertical ads]. AFP (via Gulf News): "The company says quick videos ads that fill vertical, or portrait, displays are nine times more likely to be watched than the horizontal, or landscape, ads found on most other websites and apps." So the comparison is Snapchat against other platforms, not an A/B test on one platform. Snapchat co-founded a vertical-video ad agency the same year. The Jon Steinberg Medium post often given as the origin returned 403 (both URLs), and x.com is not reachable.
Origin 2: MediaBrix press release, Nov 17, 2016 [VENDOR, in-app ad network launching vertical ads]: "vertically presented video ads were completed 90 percent of the time". This is an absolute rate for an interstitial ad, with no sample stated. Blogs mutate it into "90% higher completion rate" (search snippets) and "90% of vertical videos have a higher watch completion rate" (yansmedia.com).
Origin 3: TikTok auction-ads PDF [PLATFORM DOC]: "TikToks shot vertically have an average 25% higher 6-second watch-through rate." Ads only, no method.
Platform doc: YouTube treats orientation as a format rule, not a performance claim: videos "with a square or vertical aspect ratio up to three minutes in length will be categorized as Shorts".
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://gulfnews.com/technology/snapchat-drives-trend-toward-vertical-videos-1.1552704 -> https://www.marketingdive.com/news/vertical-video-its-not-just-for-snapchat-anymore/442440/ -> https://www.covideo.com/resources/blog/horizontal-vs-vertical-videos/ ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mediabrix-launches-vertical-video-and-360-degree-experiences-across-its-premium-in-app-ad-platform-300365013.html -> https://www.yansmedia.com/blog/vertical-video-statistics ; https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf ; https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. These are cross-platform or ad-only comparisons by companies selling vertical inventory. There was no same-content A/B test.


BELIEF 6: "Use 3-5 hashtags"
Sprout Social [VENDOR]: "Data from CapCut (ByteDance's official editor) explicitly states that using 3 to 5 hashtags gives the best results". The linked CapCut page [VENDOR, editing app, Oct 24, 2025] has no data: "Using 3 to 5 hashtags on TikTok usually gives the best results", "This number fits within the limits TikTok seems to enforce". The chain ends there.
Ignite Social Media [VENDOR, agency]: "Sprout Social (2024) reports that TikTok posts with trending hashtags have 23% higher engagement rates". There is no link, and Sprout's TikTok hashtag guide (fetched) contains no "23%". Origin NOT FOUND.
Metricool [VENDOR, analytics tool], May 2026: "Posts with at least one hashtag receive nearly 5% more views". This is observational on 2,314,756 posts and compares zero hashtags with one or more, not counts.
Platforms: TikTok, July 30, 2020: "More hashtags won't guarantee a broader reach ... hashtags like #FYP, #ForYou, and #ForYouPage work just like all other hashtags". TikTok, June 18, 2020: recommendations use "Video information, which might include details like captions, sounds, and hashtags". Instagram: Digital Trends (Dec 19, 2025) quotes Mosseri: posts "capped at five hashtags" and hashtags "don't necessarily increase a post's reach". That five is a cap, not a measured optimum. help.instagram.com returned 400.
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-hashtags/ -> https://www.capcut.com/resource/how-many-hashtags-should-i-use-on-tiktok (no data) ; https://www.ignitesocialmedia.com/social-media-marketing/hashtags-in-2025-are-they-still-relevant-for-social-media-marketing/ (dead end) ; https://metricool.com/press-release-tiktok-study-2026/ ; https://newsroom.tiktok.com/5-tips-for-tiktok-creators?lang=en ; https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you?lang=en ; https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/instagram-now-limits-you-to-five-hashtags-per-post/
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? n/a - no measurement exists for the count "N". The one measurement found (Metricool, any versus none) is observational and does not control for topic.


BELIEF 7: "Put a face (and text) in the thumbnail"
YouTube Help [PLATFORM DOC]: "Fun fact: 90% of the best-performing videos have custom thumbnails." No method, no base rate, no date. On faces it only advises: casual-viewer content "might focus on actions and emotions that are more universally relatable, like a shocked face". On text: "If you add text, make sure to use a font that's easy to read." No number for either.
YouTube's own A/B tool [PLATFORM DOC] picks winners by watch time, not CTR: "we optimize tests for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through-rate". Third-party tools "often only optimize for click-through rate, which may determine a different 'winner'". For short video specifically: "A/B testing is not available for Shorts."
thumbnailtest.com [VENDOR, sells thumbnail A/B testing]: "You've seen people do it, I've seen people do it... Everybody has! And that's really enough proof to understand that faces work for thumbnails."
vidIQ "YouTube Thumbnail Study" [VENDOR, sells YouTube growth tools] could not be checked: curl returned 429 and WebFetch returned 403. Its figures (faces in 69% of 500 "breakouts") appear only in search snippets and are NOT used here.
Short-video relevance: the Shorts feed autoplays, so no thumbnail is chosen at the point of viewing. NOT FOUND: any measurement of thumbnail effects on Shorts, TikTok or Reels watch.
SOURCE: [PLATFORM DOC] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en ; https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en ; https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/face-in-youtube-thumbnail/ ; https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/ (fetch failed)
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? n/a - no measurement exists (the 90% custom-thumbnail line is descriptive and has no method)


BELIEF 8: "Captions increase watch time by 12% / 80% more likely to finish"
Origin of 12%: the same Facebook for Business page (Feb 2016) [PLATFORM DOC]: "Internal tests show that captioned video ads increase video view time by an average of 12%". Footnote 4 is attached; its text is not on the page. Ads only. The page also carries an advertiser testimonial: "Adding captions increased watch time by 25%" (A&W Canada).
Repeaters: Social Media Today (Feb 13, 2016), AdExchanger (Feb 10, 2016), Rev [VENDOR, sells captioning] ("A Facebook study also found that captions increase viewing time on video ads by an average of 12%"), Instapage [VENDOR]. Rev links Digiday for this; Digiday's page has no 12% figure.
Origin of 80%: Verizon Media and Publicis Media [VENDOR, ad sellers]. Streaming Media: "an online survey of 5,616 U.S. adults age 18 to 54 conducted in April". 3Play Media [VENDOR, sells captioning]: "80% say they're more likely to watch an entire video with captions." That is stated intention in a survey, not measured watch time. The Forbes copy returned 403.
Contrary evidence in the chain: Digiday (2016) quotes agency MEC that "brand lift and intent to purchase were not affected by whether the viewer watched the video with the sound on or off".
SOURCE: [PLATFORM DOC] https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads -> https://www.adexchanger.com/digital-tv/facebook-adds-viewable-video-options-automated-captions-autoplay/ -> https://www.socialmediatoday.com/social-business/facebook-adds-automated-captions-video-ads-offers-tips-improve-video-performance -> https://www.rev.com/resources/how-to-add-captions-subtitles-to-facebook-video-ads ; https://instapage.com/blog/closed-captioning-mute-videos ; https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/ReadArticle.aspx?ArticleID=131860 -> https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? partly. Facebook calls it a test (implying comparison) but publishes no design. Verizon's figure is a survey. Neither is organic short video.


BELIEF 9: "85% of (social) video is watched without sound"
Origin: Digiday, May 17, 2016 (trade press reporting publisher self-reports): "As much as 85 percent of video views happen with the sound off, according to multiple publishers." The publishers are LittleThings ("Eighty-five percent of its viewership"), Mic ("85 percent of its 30-second views") and PopSugar ("between 50 and 80 percent"). That is two Facebook publishers in 2016.
Mutations: Rev [VENDOR]: "a staggering 85% of people watch Facebook videos without sound". Instapage [VENDOR] calls it "Facebook's '85% of video is watched without sound' statistic". Tubefilter: "85% of Facebook's video views". Publisher views became people, and then a Facebook statistic.
Contrary platform doc: TikTok's ad PDF: "The second you open TikTok, it's a sound-on experience." No TikTok number is given.
Verdict: this is a 2016 Facebook-feed self-report from two publishers, applied today to sound-on short-video apps.
SOURCE: [VENDOR] https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/ -> https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/publishers-say-85-percent-of-facebook-video-is-watched-without-sound/ -> https://www.tubefilter.com/2016/05/17/85-percent-facebook-video-views-without-volume/ -> https://www.rev.com/resources/how-to-add-captions-subtitles-to-facebook-video-ads ; https://instapage.com/blog/closed-captioning-mute-videos ; contra https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? no. This is publisher-reported aggregate share, with no method.


BELIEF 10: "Attention spans are 8 seconds, shorter than a goldfish, so cut fast"
Chain: houseofmarketers.com [VENDOR] links Time (May 14, 2015, Kevin McSpadden): "You Now Have a Shorter Attention Span Than a Goldfish". Time (fetched via WebFetch; curl got 406) describes Microsoft Canada researchers who "surveyed 2,000 participants and studied the brain activity of 112 others". vidpros.com [VENDOR] repeats it: "attention span has shrunk to less than that of a goldfish".
Traced by BBC More or Less (Simon Maybin, 10 March 2017): "the figure ... did not actually come from Microsoft's research. It appears in the report, but with a citation for another source called Statistic Brain". When the sources Statistic Brain lists were contacted, "neither can find any record of research that backs up the stats." Expert: the idea of an "average attention span" is "pretty meaningless. It's very much task-dependent." Also: "There is no evidence human attentions spans are shrinking."
The chain runs: vendor blog -> Time -> Microsoft Canada report (not fetched; it is not linked from Time's text) -> Statistic Brain -> nothing.
SOURCE: [VENDOR] Statistic Brain (origin, no research found) -> Microsoft Canada 2015 report -> https://time.com/3858309/attention-spans-goldfish/ -> https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/ ; https://vidpros.com/video-clip-length/ ; debunk https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790
SEPARATED FROM TOPIC? n/a - no measurement exists
